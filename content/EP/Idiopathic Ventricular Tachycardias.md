- "Idiopathic VT" is essentially [[Ventricular Tachycardia (VT)|VT]] that occurs in the setting of a structurally normal heart, i.e. absence of ischemia, valvulopathies, or other cardiomyopathies, etc.
- [[Ablation|Catheter ablation]] can be used to treat and potentially provide a cure in patients that are intolerant to medications or when these are ineffective.
- Patients generally are not at an increased risk of sudden death and do not require implantation of an ICD.
- Idiopathic VT can arise from anywhere in the heart, but certain regions are more common than others.

# Outflow Tract Tachycardias

## [[Right Ventricular Outflow Tract (RVOT)|RVOT]] VT

- Structurally normal hearts
- Rarely associated with [[Sudden Cardiac Death (SCD)|SCD]] 
- Most of these patients have palpitations, lightheadedness, and syncope.
- Triggers
	- Men: exercise or catecholamine-related
	- Women: hormonal, e.g. can be around perimenopausal or peri-pregnancy periods. 
- >10% of arrhythmia burden can be associated with development of cardiomyopathy
	- Keep 👀 out to detect for LV dysfunction
- Mechanism
	- [[Tachyarrhythmias#Delayed afterdepolarizations (DADs)|DADs]]: at slower rates, there is a little calcium overload in the cell. At faster HR, and one of these calcium overload reach the activation threshold → generates a QRS complex.
- Work-up
	- Exclude [[Arrhythmogenic Right Ventricular Cardiomyopathy (ARVC)|ARVC]] as a cause of the arrythmia
- Management
	- Calcium overload is suppressed by [[Beta-blockers|BBs]], [[Calcium Channel Blockers (CCBs)|CCBs]], and RVOT *uniquely* can also be suppressed by [[Adenosine|adenosine]]. 
		- Remember, [[Adenosine|adenosine]] not only suppress this SVT by causing [[Atrioventricular Block|AV block]], but can also suppress RVOT ectopy.
		- These 💊 ↓ the "calcium sparks" that can trigger RVOT VT.
