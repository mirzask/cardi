- Clinical definition by HR criteria
	- Sinus rate > 100 bpm at rest, or
	- mean 24-hour HR >90 bpm
	- *excluding* or causes of [[Sinus Tachycardia|sinus tachycardia]]
	- Associated with distressing symptoms of palpitations ("syndrome")
- sometimes confused with [[Postural Orthostatic Tachycardia Syndrome (POTS)|POTS]]
	- unlike [[Postural Orthostatic Tachycardia Syndrome (POTS)|POTS]] (where tachycardia in POTS is triggered by orthostatic stress), IST occurs independent of body position 
	- POTS patients usually have a normal resting *supine* HR
	- both predominately affecting young women. 
- The key 🔑 to making a diagnosis of IST is ambulatory monitoring; IST patients, unlike patients with [[Postural Orthostatic Tachycardia Syndrome (POTS)|POTS]], demonstrate **relative night-time supine tachycardia**. 
	- Of course, other common supraventricular tachycardias may need to be excluded, though these generally have a distinct onset and offset of symptoms and are not primarily postural in presentation.

# Work-up

- H&P (1)
- Orthostatic Vital Signs (1)
- 12 lead ECG (1)
- CBC, Thyroid Function (2a)
- 24h [[Ambulatory ECG Monitoring#Holter Monitor|Holter Monitor]] (2a)
- Urine/Serum Drug Screen (2b)
- Autonomic Testing (2b)
- [[Exercise ECG|Exercise Stress Test]] (2b)
