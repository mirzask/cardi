# Coronary evaluation

- Generate orthogonal views of coronary arteries using **MPR** (e.g. 1 mm thickness)
- Align 2 view through the long axis
- Then double click on the remaining plane to see the SAX and evaluate for luminal stenosis

## 'C' view of RCA


> [!tip] Useful to view RCA and LCx
> Both the RCA and LCx lie along the atrioventricular groove, so the 'C' view is useful to evaluate the RCA and LCx. When you get your RCA lined up (as below) in the MIP projection, you can scroll along and assess the LCx as well.

- Coronal view: Position crosshair (⌖) at mid-RCA
- Axial view: center on the SAX view of the RCA
	- rotate ⌖ in the axial view until you are aligned parallel to the ventricular septum on your coronal window
- In your axial view, rotate ⌖ to align the orthogonal view (e.g. coronal window) so that the orthogonal view is along the atrioventricular groove
- Switch to MIP view
	- MIP will help give you an overview of the vessel. Once you identify some areas of concern → switch to thin-slice images for closer investigation 🔍 and evaluation for stenosis.


> [!warning] Avoid assessing diameter stenosis using thick-slab MIP
> When quantifying stenosis, you will always want to use thin slice images.

# Cardiac Chamber evaluation

## Left Heart

- On **axial view**, line up the ⌖ so that it is centered at the level of the [[Mitral Valve|mitral valve]] and the line goes through the LV apex
- In the remaining window where the LV looks like it is in the **2-chamber view**, also line up the ⌖ so that it is centered at the level of the [[Mitral Valve|mitral valve]] and the line goes through the LV apex

![[How to Read a Gated CTA Chest-1754681378171.webp]]

- The remaining third window is now our **short axis (SAX)** view → scrolling through this window gives you the SAX orientation of the LV (similar to [[Cardiac MRI]])
	- Scroll through from apex → base and examine the LV cavity, filling defects, structural abnormalities, integrity of the ventricular septum
- If you continue to scroll up, you'll be at the level of the [[Left Atrium]]

## [[Left Atrial Appendage]]

- Open your 2-chamber (2CH) view (aka vertical long axis/VLA)
	- Also provides a different view to assess the [[Mitral Valve|MV leaflets]]
- Scroll through to assess for any filling defect

## Right Heart

- Open your 4-chamber (4CH) view (aka horizontal long axis/HLA)
- The [[Right Ventricle|RV]] will have a prominent moderator band and relatively greater trabeculations (compared to the [[Left Ventricle|LV]])
- The [[Tricuspid Valve|tricuspid valve]] may be difficult to closely evaluate
- When inspecting the [[Right Atrium|RA]], you may see a whisp of contrast entering from the [[Left Atrium|LA]] with flow that is parallel in direction to the interatrial septum suggestive of [[Patent Foramen Ovale|PFO]]
- Scrolling further down
	- Coronary sinus extending into the [[Right Atrium|RA]]
	- Inferior cavoatrial junction
	- IVC

## Pulmonary veins → [[Left Atrium]]

- While still in your 4-chamber (4CH) view (aka horizontal long axis/HLA), scroll up/down to assess the pulmonary veins to see that they are draining into the [[Left Atrium|LA]]

# Pericardial evaluation

- After resetting your images, use your **axial images** and scroll up/down
- Look for:
	- pericardial thickening
		- ≥1.5-2 mm on thin section images would be questionably abnormal
	- pericardial calcification
	- [[Pericardial Effusion|pericardial effusion]]

# Great Vessels

## Ascending Aorta

- To create a **short axis** of the [[Aortic Root|aortic root]] →
- Center your ⌖ at the level of the [[Aortic Valve|AV]] in 2 orthogonal views along the long axis of the [[aortic root]]
	- ![[How to Read a Gated CTA Chest-1754682755650.webp]]

## Pulmonary artery

- Reset, then in your axial view → find your main pulmonary artery and align the ⌖ at the center of the main PA. Get your SAX to make measurement by aligning 2 orthogonal views along the long axis of the main PA.
- In general, the main pulmonary artery should measure <2.9 cm in diameter.
