- The E/A ratio in healthy, euvolemic, recumbent young adults is typically >1.
- Valsalva maneuver can be helpful to distinguish a "pseudonormal" mitral inflow pattern (grade II diastolic dysfunction) from normal diastolic function. 
	- In patients with normal diastolic function, the Valsalva maneuver will lead to a ↓ in the amplitude of both the E-wave and the A-wave due to ↓ **preload**. 
	- In patients with grade II diastolic dysfunction, by ↓ preload with the Valsalva maneuver, filling will occur on the *flatter* portion of the LV diastolic pressure–volume relationship; thus, whereas E-wave amplitude ↓, A-wave amplitude falls *less* and may actually increase, resulting in E/A reversal (i.e., E/A ratio <1).
